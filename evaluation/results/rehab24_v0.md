# REHAB24-6 arm abduction: Arm out to the side on real video

`rehab24_v0` · regenerated by `evaluation/scripts/rehab24_eval.py` on 2026-10-11 (OpenCV 5.0.0, Python 3.12.14, Darwin arm64); pipeline fingerprint `064db736f8da` (SHA-256 of the perception sources that produce the angle). **Do not hand-edit**: every number is in `rehab24_v0.json`.

## Honest reading

- **Real RGB video of 9 healthy adults (25–50 y) in a motion-capture lab, against a motion-capture reference.** These are the first real-video numbers for Arm out to the side. The subjects are not patients. The cameras are lab cameras in the corners of an 8.2 × 7 m room, several metres from a standing subject in a black mocap suit: Camera17 with the subject facing it is close to our front view, but it is **not a laptop webcam at chest height 1.5–2 m away**, and nobody is seated.
- The headline reference is anatomical abduction in the subject's frontal plane (`ref_frontal`). Our angle is a 2D, camera-relative number, so this agreement holds for a front-on view; off-axis views are reported separately and are worse.
- Ex1's lifts go overhead (front-view rep peaks 79.1–161.8°, median 124.3°; every rep labelled correct ≥ 100.4°), past the 90° the product prescribes, and no repetition holds for 3 s. So the product counts the reps but marks most of them **unverified (`hold_too_short`) by design**, raises `above_ceiling`, and its shoulder-hike flag fires on almost every rep (see below). The rep F1 counts every rep the product reported, verified or not; the movement F1 drops the hold rule.
- Thresholds were **not** tuned on this data. Two pose-stage defects it exposed were fixed (below), and the numbers in this file are after those fixes; the before/after table shows what they changed.

- **Why `ref_cam` differs by ~10°.** `ref_cam` applies our formula (angle at the shoulder between elbow and same-side hip) to the mocap *joint centres* projected into Camera17. The mocap hip joint sits ~0.1 m from the midline and the shoulder joint ~0.2 m, so that shoulder→hip line leans inward and opens the angle: on the same frames `ref_cam` − `ref_frontal` = 11.02° ± 3.2° (SD). BlazePose's hip landmark sits wider, on the body outline, so the product's line is near the trunk axis and it agrees with anatomical abduction instead. `ref_cam` is therefore a definition gap plus projection error, not the landmark error alone, and is reported for completeness only.

## Method

- **Data:** Černek, A., Sedmidubsky, J., Budikova, P.: REHAB24-6: Physical Therapy Dataset for Analyzing Pose Estimation Methods. 17th International Conference on Similarity Search and Applications (SISAP 2024), Springer LNCS. Data: Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.13305826, CC BY-NC 4.0. Ex1 = sideways raising of the straightened right arm; 13 recordings of 9 subjects; 178 segmented repetitions (start/end frame at 30 fps), each labelled correct/incorrect, with the subject's orientation toward Camera17 (per repetition: five recordings turn from front to half-profile halfway) and a lights-on/off label. Camera18 is orthogonal to Camera17 (Segmentation.txt), so a front view for Camera17 is a profile for Camera18.
- **Product run:** each video decoded with `cv2.VideoCapture`, every 2nd frame (15 fps, the top of the product's 10–15 fps stream), scaled to fit 640 × 480 (aspect kept: Camera17 → 640 × 360, Camera18 → 270 × 480), JPEG q80 round-trip as the browser sends, then `SessionPipeline(mode="arm", exercise="abduction", side="right")` with every threshold at its product default. Only the dose is lifted to 1 × 999 so counting does not stop after the shipped 2 × 8 (no detection logic depends on the dose). Timestamps = frame index / 30 s, the dataset's own clock.
- **References** (per 30 fps frame; the joint files and the videos have the same frame count and the segmentation indexes both): `ref_frontal` = angle between the upper arm (`RightArm` → `RightForeArm`) and the trunk-down vector (`Neck` → mid-hip of `LeftUpLeg`/`RightUpLeg`), both projected onto the subject's frontal plane (spanned by the shoulder-to-shoulder axis and the trunk axis, so it follows the subject, not the room); `ref_3d` = the same vectors unprojected (elevation in any plane); `ref_cam` = our formula on the mocap joints projected into the recording camera (the authors' estimated pinhole model).
- **Frame errors** use frames inside labelled repetitions with a pose and an open gate. Frames are serially correlated, so the per-rep peak numbers (one per repetition: the product's maximum over the repetition vs the reference maximum) are the fairer sample; a rep peak needs ≥ 50 % of the repetition's frames scored.
- **Rep F1 (state machine):** every `rep_complete` (verified or not), one-to-one against labelled repetitions, matched when the intervals overlap by ≥ 50 % of the shorter. **Movement F1:** a lift is a rise of ≥ 20° to a peak ≥ 40° then a fall of ≥ 20° in the product angle (pose frames, gate ignored), matched when the peak lies inside a labelled repetition. The second shows what the angle signal supports without the hold and start-pose rules; the first is what a patient gets. Predictions in a recording that changes orientation are assigned to the view of the repetition containing them (or the nearest).
