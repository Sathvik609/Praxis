# Praxis — Technical Report

> Status: **Phase 0–8 (16 Sep 2026)**. Every section is filled in the same commit that ships the feature it describes. Anything simulated, rule-based or fallback is labeled here, in the UI, and in the trace.

## 1. Problem and clinical framing
Adherence and coaching aid for therapist-prescribed upper-limb rehab at home. Not a medical device. The therapist prescribes; the coach proposes within the prescription; hard-coded stop rules override the coach. Three camera-coached tasks (NHPT, Waiter – cup, Arm to the front); all other GRASP exercises are instructions-only.

## 2. System architecture
One Python 3.12 process: FastAPI + WebSocket frame ingest (640×480 JPEG from the browser) → OpenCV 5 perception → stop rules → coach (rule-based first, Strands/Bedrock agent in W6) → JSON events, captions, overlay geometry back to the browser. Raw frames never leave the process. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-16-praxis-rebuild-design.md`.

**Frame stream and back-pressure (Sprint D, 17 Sep).** The browser prefixes every JPEG with its capture time (8-byte little-endian float64, ms since epoch from `performance.timeOrigin + performance.now()`; `web/src/session/latency.ts`, `praxis_session/latency.py`) and sends the next frame only after the service answered the previous one with its per-frame event (`frame`, or `setup_checks` in calibrate mode; 250 ms safety timeout), so `fps` is a ceiling and a slow host gets fewer, fresher frames instead of a growing queue. On the service a reader task feeds one queue; `pipeline.process` runs in the default thread pool (OpenCV releases the GIL) and any frames that queued up meanwhile are dropped oldest-first (`frame.dropped`, with `frame_index` still counting every received frame). Every `frame`/`heartbeat` event carries `t_capture` and `latency = {capture_to_event_ms, proc_ms}`; every `coach` event that follows perception events carries `latency = {event_to_cue_ms, capture_to_cue_ms}`; the live screens show `latency p50/p95 · capture→event · event→cue` over a rolling window of 60, and `summary.latency` (kept in `main.py`, merged into the summary and the stored record) carries the session percentiles shown on the report. Replay keeps clip time and reports `proc_ms` only. `capture_to_event_ms` compares the browser clock with the service clock: exact on the laptop (one clock), skew-inclusive when the service is remote. Setup sockets (`mode=calibrate`) create no coach session, and a trace file is only opened on the first non-header row.

## 3. Perception (OpenCV 5.0.0, `opencv-python-headless==5.0.0.93`)

### 3.1 Sheets and calibration (ArUco → homography → mm)
`perception/board_spec.py` is the single source of truth for both printed sheets: the NHPT board sheet (A4 portrait, ArUco DICT_4X4_50 ids 0–3 of 40 mm at known centres, nine 10 mm hole rings at 32 mm pitch) and the Waiter – cup target sheet (A4 landscape, same markers, five 20 mm dots on a 250 mm-radius arc spanning ±26°, ~219 mm chord). `perception/render.py` draws the canonical sheet with `cv2.aruco.generateImageMarker` and `cv2.circle`; `tools/make_sheets.py` writes `docs/sheets/*.pdf` at true scale.

`perception/calibration.py`: `cv2.aruco.ArucoDetector` with sub-pixel corner refinement finds the four markers; their 16 corners (sheet mm from the spec) go to `cv2.findHomography` (frame px → sheet mm). The **first** calibration of a session needs all four markers (the setup screen's "Sheet found" row and Continue require it). After that (Sprint D-CAL-1, 17 Sep) a running session tolerates the forearm or a cup covering a corner: with exactly three markers a `partial` homography is fitted from their 12 corners with `cv2.RANSAC` (3 px); with fewer than three the last fresh/partial homography is `held` for up to 1.5 s (`age_s` says how stale it is); after that the frame is uncalibrated. Every `calibration` event carries `source: fresh | partial | held | null`, `age_s`, the missing ids, per-marker corners and the RMS reprojection error. On synthetic frames (`perception/synth.py`: pinhole projection with tilt 20°, yaw 8°, Gaussian noise σ 3) the RMS is about 0.2 mm and target positions round-trip within 0.5 mm; a three-marker fit round-trips within 0.5 mm on the same render. **Real-camera accuracy is unmeasured** until the first recording.

**Camera angle (D-CAL-3).** The sheet-plane homography ignores height: a peg top or a hand 30–100 mm above the sheet projects tens of mm off when the webcam looks across the table, against 5–25 mm gates. `calibration.py` therefore also runs `cv2.solvePnP` (planar `SOLVEPNP_IPPE`) on the visible marker corners (sheet plane z = 0) with a nominal pinhole (f = 1.2 × frame width, principal point at the centre, no distortion) and reports `tilt_deg`, the angle between the optical axis and the sheet normal (arccos |R₃₃| after `cv2.Rodrigues`). It is a setup gate, not a measurement: on synthetic renders it lands within 1° of the rendered angle for both sheets from 0° to 50° despite the nominal focal length. Screen 05 gains a required row "Camera looks down at the sheet" (`angle`, pass ≤ 25°) with an overhead-mount diagram, and the `quality` event carries the `angle` block. A held result carries the last measured tilt.

### 3.2 Setup quality checks
`perception/quality.py` feeds the five rows of wireframe screen 05 plus the camera-angle row added in Sprint D: camera permission (browser-side), one person in view, hand and task area in view (all targets project inside the frame with a 16 px margin), sheet found (all four markers; a partial or held fix does not pass it), camera looks down at the sheet (`tilt_deg` ≤ 25°, see §3.1), lighting and steady tracking (mean luma 60–200, variance of Laplacian ≥ 40, mean marker-corner jitter < 2 px over 10 frames). "One person in view" is a **frame-difference motion fallback** (`method: frame_difference`) until the pose model lands in Phase 5; it is not a required check for Continue.

### 3.3 Pipeline and events
`pipeline.py` composes calibration and quality per frame into `calibration`, `quality`, `setup_checks` events (validated against `events.schema.json`). Twenty consecutive uncalibrated frames after a calibrated one (`LOSS_FRAMES`, on top of the calibrator's 1.5 s hold, so roughly 3 s at 12 fps) emit `perception_lost` (with missing ids); recovery emits `perception_regained`. One pipeline per WebSocket connection; frames are dropped after processing.

### 3.4 Board state (warp, Hough holes, HSV pegs, masked template)
`perception/board.py`. `cv2.warpPerspective` maps the frame through the calibration homography to a canonical 3 px/mm top-down view of a 140 mm square around the hole grid. Holes are found once with `cv2.HoughCircles` and cached; each circle is snapped to the nearest expected hole (≤ 4 mm) and any hole Hough misses falls back to the spec position (`hole_source` = hough | mixed | spec is reported in every event). **The Hough centre is where occupancy is measured**: the per-hole disc is drawn on it, so a grid printed or placed a few mm off the spec is scored where the hole really is (test: a grid shifted 2 mm still reads a seated peg at > 0.6 fill). At detection time a grey empty-hole template is captured per hole from the same view (holes that are not clearly empty get none; `reset_holes()` re-captures).
Pegs are segmented with `cv2.inRange` in HSV and blobbed with `cv2.findContours`. The default mask is "saturated, not sheet-white and **not skin**": S ≥ 90, V ≥ 60 minus the skin band H 0–25 (OpenCV units, i.e. 0–50°), S 40–170, V 80–255 — which also removes a light wooden board or peg tint, so a skin patch over the grid never flips a hole and a wooden board is not a field of pegs. Wooden or pale pegs need **tap-to-sample** (Sprint D-SEG-1): `sample_peg_colour` takes a 6 mm patch where the patient tapped (`sample_colour` WebSocket command, by hole index or sheet mm) and sets a ±12° hue window (wrapped around red) with S/V floors at 60 % of the medians; the skin band stays excluded unless the sampled colour could itself read as skin (hue in 0–25 and median S ≤ 190). The range is reported in every `frame.board.colour` and in the command reply for the trace. The hand's bounding box from the hand tracker (expanded 15 mm) is excluded from the **free-peg blobs only** — never from hole occupancy, because the hand covers a hole right after seating a peg.
Occupancy per hole is the fraction of peg mask inside the disc (≥ 0.35 → occupied). **The masked `cv2.matchTemplate` score is a tie-breaker**: when the fraction is within ±0.1 of the threshold, the hole is empty iff the masked `TM_CCOEFF_NORMED` score against its captured template is ≥ 0.75 (measured on synthetic frames: empty ≈ 0.99, seated peg ≈ 0.61, peg hovering 5 mm off the hole ≈ 0.81); outside the tie zone the fraction alone decides. (The previous synthetic filled-disc template under a disc mask scored ≈ 0.03 for empty and occupied alike — it carried no information; the audit was right that it was ignored.) Free pegs (blobs not in any hole) are reported in mm. The NHPT occupancy debounce is **time-based** (0.25 s converted to frames from the observed frame gaps, frame count as a floor), so a 100 ms flicker is ignored at 12 fps and 30 fps alike. **Limitation:** all thresholds are tuned on synthetic renders and printed flat rings; the physical board with recessed holes and real lighting is Phase 9.

### 3.4.1 Peg FSM and NHPT timing
`perception/peg_fsm.py`: `at_source → grasped → in_transit → over_target → seated → hand_clear`. Occupancy is debounced (0.25 s, see §3.4). **Grasp** fires either when a free peg disappears (pegs laid on the sheet) **or** (Sprint D-SEG-2) when the hand's pinch aperture is ≤ 25 mm while the hand is in the source zone — off the hole grid, or within 40 mm of a free peg — for two consecutive observations, so pegs lifted from a dish, where no free peg is ever visible, still start the timer; `state_change.grasp` records which (`count` | `pinch`). In-transit = a free peg or the hand is > 20 mm from where it was picked up; over-target = a free peg or hand within 10 mm of an empty hole; seated = a hole becomes occupied; hand-clear = hand > 25 mm from the hole (or, without hand landmarks, no free peg within 15 mm for `clear_frames`). **Seating is hand-gated**: once hand data has been seen in the session, a hole that fills without the hand having been within 30 mm of it in the last 0.5 s is still counted, but its `rep_complete` carries `verified: false, reason: "no_hand_near_hole"` (a hovering hand or a phantom blob cannot seat a hole on its own; unverified is not failed). Without any hand data reps stay `verified: true` as before. Events: `state_change`, `rep_complete` (grasp/seat/clear timestamps, `verified`, `reason`), `wrong_hole`, `drop` (a free peg stationary for 1.5 s without hand data, 0.3 s when the hand is visibly away). The `frame.overlay.next_hole` hint is `null` in any-hole mode: the test does not prescribe an order, so the UI draws no amber ring.
`perception/nhpt.py` runs the FSM in any-hole mode: the timer starts at the first `grasped` transition and stops at the ninth `seated`; per-peg splits are seated-to-seated. The FSM keeps running after completion so the final `rep_complete` still fires.

### 3.4.2 Pipeline, events, replay
`pipeline.py` mode `nhpt` emits one `frame` event per frame (calibrated flag, NHPT snapshot, occupancy, hole/marker geometry in frame pixels, suggested next hole) plus every FSM/NHPT event; the FSM is not stepped while uncalibrated, and the Phase 1 perception-lost/regained logic applies. `replay.py` drives the identical pipeline from a video with timestamps from the file fps, so results are deterministic.
### 3.5 Hand (ONNX via `cv2.dnn`)
`perception/hand.py` runs the OpenCV Zoo MediaPipe pair (Apache-2.0, recorded in `docs/LICENSES.md` before download; SHA256 pinned in `tools/fetch_models.py`): palm detection at 192×192 then hand landmarks at 224×224, both through `cv2.dnn.readNet` / `forward` (≈ 3–6 ms per stage on an Apple-silicon laptop CPU). The Zoo pre/post-processing is vendored verbatim under `perception/zoo/`. The best-scoring palm yields 21 landmarks in frame pixels; through the ArUco homography they become sheet millimetres, from which we compute pinch aperture (thumb tip ↔ index tip, reported as `pinch_rel_mm`: a sheet-plane projection of a hand above the sheet — relative, not calibrated to the hand's height), index-tip path speed, spectral arc length of the speed profile (SPARC), and tremor as the dominant 3–12 Hz component of the detrended fingertip trace over a 3 s window. Sprint D-SEG-3 set the landmark confidence threshold to the Zoo default 0.5 (0.8 dropped most real frames) and lets the metrics window survive up to three consecutive missed frames (`miss_tolerance=3`) instead of being wiped by one, so SPARC and tremor can actually populate on a webcam. The thumb/index midpoint feeds `Observation.hand_mm` and the pinch feeds `Observation.pinch_mm`; the peg FSM uses them for the dish grasp, in-transit, over-target, hand-gated seating, hand-clear and the faster (0.3 s) drop rule, and the hand's bounding box (all 21 landmarks in mm) is excluded from the free-peg blobs (§3.4). When the models are absent the tracker reports `available: false`, `/health` says so, and NHPT runs on occupancy alone with every rep `verified: true`. Handedness from the model is mirrored by the webcam and is not used clinically.
### 3.6 Waiter – cup (target sheet, cup base, reach FSM)
`perception/cup.py`. The frame is warped through the calibration homography to a 2 px/mm canonical view of the whole A4 target sheet; the cup's **base band** — by default a saturated blue sticker or tape ring (HSV hue 95–130), or any colour the patient samples with the `sample_colour` command (a 6 mm patch → ±12° hue window with S/V floors, same sampler as the pegs, Sprint D-SEG-3; the range is in every `frame.cup.colour`) — is segmented with `cv2.inRange` + morphological open, the largest `cv2.findContours` blob gives the centroid and equivalent diameter in mm. `CupFSM` runs `at_dot → lifted → in_transit → over_dot → placed → hand_clear`: lifted when the base disappears or moves > 20 mm from its dot; over a dot within 25 mm; placed after `clear_frames` stationary frames; hand-clear when the hand (Phase 3 landmarks) is > 60 mm away or, without hand data, after `clear_frames` more frames. A placement that was only inferred from the hand while the base was hidden is reported **unverified (cup_occluded)** — unless the base was seen within 25 mm of the destination at any time in the last 0.6 s before hand-clear (`rest_window_s`), which is the normal case when a hand from above hides the base at the moment of release and the cup reappears as the fingers open; then the reach is **verified**. A reach during which calibration was lost (fewer than three markers for over 1.5 s, D-CAL-1) is **unverified (marker_lost)**; one covered marker no longer counts as loss. Reaches follow the arc 1→2→3→4→5→4→3→2→1; a wrong dot emits `wrong_dot` and re-anchors the sequence. Two slow arcs are followed by two fast arcs only after the patient answers the progression prompt (WebSocket command `set_phase`), never automatically; the prescription clamp lands with the coach in Phase 7. Events: `reach_complete` (from/to dot, reach ms, verified, reason, arc, phase), `arc_complete` (mean verified reach, unverified count), `cup_complete`.
### 3.7 Pose and Arm to the front
`perception/pose.py` runs the OpenCV Zoo MediaPipe person detector (224×224) and BlazePose landmarker (256×256) through `cv2.dnn` (Apache-2.0, recorded before download; vendored wrappers under `perception/zoo/`; OpenCV 5's graph engine returns the person detector's two outputs in swapped order, which `pose.py` re-sorts by width). From the 33 landmarks we compute, in the image plane, shoulder flexion (angle at the shoulder between upper arm and trunk), elbow angle, trunk lean (hip→shoulder midline from vertical, signed), and shoulder hike (practising shoulder height above the other, normalised by torso length and baselined at the start of the set). The shoulder angle is One-Euro filtered. Side view is the assumption; `view_ok` is false when the shoulders are more than 0.6 torso-lengths apart. BlazePose's per-landmark visibility is reported in three groups because they gate three different things: `visibility` (practising-side shoulder, elbow, hip — the angle), `hip_visibility` (both hips — the lean) and `shoulders_visibility` (both shoulders — the hike). **These are camera-relative progress numbers, not goniometry.**

`perception/arm_fsm.py`: `start_pose → moving → target_reached → hold → return`; target clamped to the 90° ceiling in code (`clamped` reported); a rep is verified when it reached target − 10° and held ≥ 3 s; `target_missed` and `hold_too_short` reps are counted but unverified. **Gating (Sprint D):** the FSM freezes — no transitions, no compensation events, the angle still shown — while the patient faces the camera (`view_ok` false → `arm.gate = "turn_side"` on every frame) or the practising-side joint visibility is below 0.5 (`arm.gate = "hips_out_of_view"`), so a frontal lift can never be scored as a rep and a seated patient with hips behind a desk gets no spurious flags. `compensation_lean` fires once per rep only after |lean| > 12° has persisted for 0.5 s with both hips visible (flagged after three); `shoulder_hiking` needs both shoulders visible and hike > 0.08 torso lengths above baseline. Pipeline mode `arm` needs no sheet; pose absence for 15 frames pauses with `perception_lost`. Because the DNN cannot detect a drawn figure, the evaluation feeds a synthetic 33-point landmark stream through the same FSM (`replay.run_landmark_replay`), which validates the state machine and angle code, not the detector.

## 3.9 Avatar (reference, never a scorer)
`web/src/avatar/` (Sprint B, 17 Sep). Everything is generated from primitives in code; there are no imported models, textures or pre-rendered clips.

- **Rig and kinematics** (`rig.ts`): twelve joints (hips, spine, neck, head, and shoulder → elbow → wrist → hand per arm) with a rest pose and forward kinematics returning world positions and rotations. `ik.ts` is a small Levenberg–Marquardt two-bone solver over shoulder pitch/yaw/roll and elbow flexion, with soft terms that keep the elbow below the shoulder and outside the torso, so clips are authored as *targets* on the stage and the mitt lands on the dot or hole the caption names.
- **Body** (`body.ts`, `figure.ts`): one seamless torso–neck–head shell turned with `LatheGeometry` from a 17-point profile (Chaikin-smoothed, flattened front-to-back), a dark plinth base, spherical deltoid caps, tapered upper-arm and forearm cylinders, ball joints, and mitt hands (flattened sphere + capsule thumb) that close with a `pinch` scalar; faint additive contour lines on the chest.
- **Look** (`stage.ts`): `MeshPhysicalMaterial` (aqua, clearcoat, sheen) with a mint fresnel rim injected in `onBeforeCompile`, `RoomEnvironment` PMREM lighting, hemisphere + shadow-casting key + fill + mint rim lights, ACES tone mapping, PCF shadows onto a `ShadowMaterial` disc with a faint mint ring, and the radial `#0F262B → #050E10` stage gradient from the wireframe contract.
- **Motion** (`sampler.ts`, `clips.ts`): per-channel cubic Hermite (non-uniform Catmull–Rom) with zero velocity at hold keys and extrema, anticipation dips and overshoot settles as virtual keys, a procedural breath channel (torso scale, shoulders rise), and sticky cue state (target, carried prop, parked prop, current reach segment). Clips: *idle* (breathing, ±2° sway), *nhpt* (dish → pinch → carry → seat → release × 3 holes, looping, pegs accumulate), *cup* (place on dot 1, hand to lap, then dots 1→5→1 with the cup in hand, lap), *armFront* (anticipation dip, 3 s lift to 90°, 3 s hold with elbow softening, 3 s lower; the 90° ceiling is enforced in the keys). `mirror(clip)` swaps the arms and negates yaw/roll for right-side patients.
- **Stage props and cues** (`layout.ts`, `props.ts`, `scene.ts`): the table, the printed cup sheet with its five dots on the `board_spec.py` arc (250 mm radius, ±26°), the cup, the NHPT board with nine holes and seated pegs, the peg dish, and the dashed amber 90° line. Mint = the reach in progress (tube along the hand path, rebuilt per segment), amber = the target being reached (ring on the dot / hole / ceiling end, the target dot turns amber), joint dots with additive halos for Focus / Watch / Stable markers, red reserved for stop.
- **Panel** (`AvatarPanel.tsx`): Play / Replay / Slower / Close-up / Captions; `side` mirrors the clip and the side camera; `aspect` 16/10 (Demonstration) or 4/5 capped at 400 px (Home, live reference); marker labels are ≥ 18 px chips with 56 px tap targets, offset from the torso with leader lines and stacked when they collide, moved from the single render loop without React state. One loop drives playback, captions (10 Hz), camera presets with frame-rate-independent easing and label projection; everything is disposed on unmount; a lost WebGL context shows the reference-only text on a static gradient.

Tests (`*.test.ts`, headless): rig FK, IK reach accuracy (≤ 4 mm on every dot and hole), body geometry, figure placement, sampler continuity and hold/anticipation behaviour, clip landings against the layout, mirroring, prop builders and target resolution, panel labels/aspect/side. The scene itself is checked in Chrome. The avatar plays no part in verification: reps come from OpenCV events only.

## 4. Coach
Two implementations share `coach/base.py`: the deterministic `RuleCoach` (shipped, labeled "Rule-based coach · LLM agent pending" in every decision and on screen) and, in W6, a Strands agent on Bedrock. Actions are restricted to `instruct | correct | progress | regress | rest | stop`; a `Decision` must cite the perception event ids it was made from, and the tool functions carry the names the agent will bind (`get_events`, `get_exertion_state`, `get_prescription`, `read_patient_model`, `write_session_summary`, `speak`).

**Order of operations per frame** (`session.py`): perception events → behavioural exertion update → **stop rules** (`stop_rules.py`: pain ≥ 5, new/worse symptoms at check-in, trunk lean flagged three times, three consecutive unverified reps, exertion high for 60 s, perception loss) → coach (silent while stopped or paused) → trace. Every stop/pause/rest that a clinician should see also writes a `therapist_flag`. A perception pause clears itself (D-CAL-2): when the pipeline's `perception_regained` arrives while `pause_rule == "perception"`, `CoachSession` emits `resume` (`rule: perception`, "Tracking is back. Carry on.") and the coach speaks again; pauses the patient asked for (`rule: patient`) or that another rule raised (lean, failed reps) still wait for a tap. The rule coach's progression proposal (fast arcs) is only made when the prescription's `allowed_adaptations.cup.fast_arcs` is true and the last arc had at most one unverified reach; it is a prompt the patient answers, and the phase changes only on "Yes". Its regression (hold 3 s → 2 s after three lean events) is clamped by `prescription.clamp`. Instructions are rate-limited to one per 2.5 s; corrections always pass.
**Order of operations per frame** (`session.py`): perception events → behavioural exertion update → **stop rules** (`stop_rules.py`: pain ≥ 5, new/worse symptoms at check-in, trunk lean flagged three times, three consecutive unverified reps, exertion high for 60 s, perception loss) → coach (silent while stopped or paused) → trace. Every stop/pause/rest that a clinician should see also writes a `therapist_flag`. The failed-reps rule re-arms after it fires (the next three unverified reps pause again) and its streak resets when the patient resumes, so it is not a once-per-session rule. `CoachSession` keeps an honest active-time clock: it stops on every pause or forced rest and restarts on `resume`, and the summary reports `camera.active_s` (practice only), `camera.paused_s`, `tracking_lost_events` and `tracking` (an NHPT assessment with any perception loss is `limited`, because pegs seated while tracking was lost cannot be verified; the other tasks use the unverified share). The summary also names its `clock` (`wall` for live sessions, `clip` for replay). The rule coach's progression proposal (fast arcs) is only made when the prescription's `allowed_adaptations.cup.fast_arcs` is true and the last arc had at most one unverified reach; it is a prompt the patient answers, and the phase changes only on "Yes". Its regression (hold 3 s → 2 s after three lean events) is clamped by `prescription.clamp`. Instructions are rate-limited to one per 2.5 s; corrections always pass.

**Trace** (`trace.py`): JSON lines per session under `traces/<id>.jsonl` with kinds `session, checkin, event, stop_rule, flag, decide, tool, action, answer, pain, rpe, pause, resume, feedback`; `/api/trace?session=` returns the tail and the live-session "Show trace" panel renders decide → tool → action lines under each perception event. Speech is a `speak` event captioned on screen (≥ 24 px) and voiced by the browser's SpeechSynthesis until Polly (W6).

**Exertion** (`exertion.py`) is a behavioural index only (rep-time slope, falling peak angles, tremor share, last RPE), labeled `method: behavioural` everywhere it appears. The RPE term fades: its contribution halves for every 120 s in which the behavioural signals stay low (a fresh RPE restarts the clock), so one "8/10" at check-in no longer pins the chip on *high* for the whole session; the reason string says "fading after N s of steady work" when it is.

### 3.10 Local store and replay
`store.py` is a SQLite database (`services/session/praxis.db`, git-ignored) with the tables DynamoDB will mirror: `patients`, `prescriptions` (versioned JSON), `sessions` (summary, check-in and feedback JSON, status, tracking = usable | limited when more than 30 % of reps were unverified), `events` (non-frame events only), `flags` (therapist flags with reviewed state), `goal_log`, `nhpt_trend`. It is seeded once with the wireframe's illustrative history (Alex M., Dr. Rao, program v3, six NHPT assessments 68 → 41.2 s, four sessions, two flags); every seeded number is labeled illustrative in the UI and carries `illustrative: true` in the stored summary, so `progress.practice` (sessions done, active hours with rests excluded) and the therapist's `adherence_pct` are computed from real sessions only; the wireframe's practice card numbers are served separately as `progress.sample` with `illustrative: true`, and `planned` / `adherence_pct` are `null` rather than invented until the prescription carries a schedule. A live session is persisted when the browser sends `feedback` after the post-session overlay; the report is then served by id. Replay sessions run on clip time, so the store dates them by when they were played (start = now, end = now + clip duration) instead of 1970. **Replay** is the judge fallback: `WS /session?mode=…&replay=<clip id>` makes the service read the recorded clip with `cv2.VideoCapture` and push it through the identical pipeline and coach at the file's frame rate while still accepting commands; the browser shows a Replay badge instead of "Live · not recording" and no frames travel to it. Clips come from `evaluation/dataset_v0/manifest.json`.

### 6.1 dataset_v0 (16 Sep 2026) — synthetic only
`evaluation/results/dataset_v0.md`, regenerated by `evaluation/scripts/eval.py` with the pipeline at **product defaults** (`debounce_frames=2`, `clear_frames=3`, the values `main.py` ships). ## 8. Privacy and safety
Video processed in memory only; never stored by default. Only events, metrics, logs leave the device. Clips only with explicit consent, 30-day deletion. Feeling is self-reported, never read from the face; face analysis limited to presence.

## 9. Limitations and honest scope
Exercise ceiling: NHPT, Waiter – cup, Arm to the front. Angles are camera-relative progress, not goniometry. NHPT is a timing aid, not a clinical instrument. Everything so far is validated on synthetic renders only.
